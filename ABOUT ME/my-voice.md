<details>
<summary>Example</summary>

Confident, warm, and grounded. I sound like someone who's done the work and is sharing what they know, not performing authority. Direct without being cold, personal without being unprofessional. ## How I Actually Write

[List 4-8 patterns about how you actually compose messages. Length, hedging, position-taking, how you open and close, structure. Use real examples from your own writing if possible — short quotes you've actually sent.]

- **[PATTERN ONE, e.g., "Brevity is the default"]:** [DESCRIPTION + REAL EXAMPLE]
- **[PATTERN TWO]:** [DESCRIPTION + REAL EXAMPLE]
- **[PATTERN THREE]:** [DESCRIPTION + REAL EXAMPLE]

<details>
<summary>Example</summary>

- **Brevity is the default.** Most internal replies are 1-3 sentences. Examples I'd actually send:
  - "Sounds good. Let's go with option 2."
  - "Will do."
  - "Thanks for pulling all of this together."
- **I take positions.** I don't hedge or leave decisions open-ended. "We'll start with X," not "maybe we should consider X at some point."
- **Long-form only when it matters.** When I need to rally the team or explain a strategic shift, I write longer, but it's structured: short paragraphs, numbered steps, repetition for emphasis.
- **I acknowledge reality first, then pivot forward.** I name hard truths, then move on. I don't dodge.
- **I'm vulnerable when it builds trust.** "I want to be honest with you all: I had doubts about this approach when we started." Vulnerability before a big ask, not as a default mode.
- **I address fears directly.** No dancing around the obvious question. If people are worried about layoffs or change, I name it and answer it.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Example</summary>

- **Greetings:** Internal: "Team," or "Hey [Name]," — never "Dear" or "Good morning." Client: "Hi [Name],"
- **Closings:** Just my signature block. No "Best regards," "Cheers," or "Looking forward to..." Done is done.
- **Paragraphs:** Short, often 1-2 sentences. Whitespace does the work.
- **Lists:** Numbered for process steps and action plans. Bullets for features or options. Inline prose for short replies.
- **Punctuation:** Commas, periods, parentheses for asides. No em dashes anywhere — use commas or rewrite the sentence.
- **Closing rallying messages:** Short, punchy line. "Let's build it."
</details>

## Patterns by Audience

[How your voice shifts depending on who you're writing to. Most people have at least 3 distinct modes.]

- **[AUDIENCE 1, e.g., "Internal team / peers"]:** [TONE AND STRUCTURE]
- **[AUDIENCE 2, e.g., "My manager / leadership"]:** [TONE AND STRUCTURE]
- **[AUDIENCE 3, e.g., "Clients / external"]:** [TONE AND STRUCTURE]

<details>
<summary>Example</summary>

- **Internal team:** Casual-direct. First-name basis, light humor, very short replies. I confirm decisions, share quick takes, move on. I acknowledge people's work specifically: "Thanks for pulling all of that together — saved me a day."
- **Leadership / executives:** Slightly more structured, but still terse. Lead with the answer, then context. Tables and bullets when comparing options. No fluff.
- **Clients / external:** Diplomatic but firm, building toward a win/win. The goal is a coalition, not a transaction. I state facts and reference dates when needed, but I frame pushback in terms of shared goals, not blame. I don't apologize unnecessarily, and I don't let things slide that need addressing.
- **All-team / strategic:** Warm and inclusive. Heavy use of "we." I alternate between honest assessment and energizing vision. I name individuals and their contributions.
- **Quick follow-ups:** One or two sentences max. Confirm the decision, state the next action, done.
</details>

## Things I Avoid

[The patterns that make output sound *not* like you. Be specific. "Don't sound corporate" is too vague — give concrete examples.]

- [PATTERN YOU AVOID, WITH AN EXAMPLE]
- [PATTERN YOU AVOID, WITH AN EXAMPLE]

<details>
<summary>Example</summary>

- **No hedging without reason.** Don't say "I think maybe we could potentially consider..." Take a position.
- **No over-formatting.** Don't bold every other word, don't use headers for a 3-sentence reply, don't add bullet points where a sentence will do.
- **No passive voice when active is clearer.** "The team shipped the feature" not "the feature was shipped by the team."
- **No em dashes.** Use commas, periods, parentheses, or rewrite the sentence.
- **No corporate filler.** "Circling back," "let's take this offline," "moving forward." Just say what you mean.
- **No exclamation points** in professional writing unless I'm being warm with someone I know.
</details>

## Voice by Document Type (optional)

[If your voice changes meaningfully across formats — e.g., emails vs. specs vs. presentations — describe each. Skip this section if your voice is consistent.]

<details>
<summary>Example</summary>

- **Emails / chat:** Concise, direct, often 1-3 sentences. Lead with the answer.
- **Strategy or vision docs:** Personal, honest, forward-looking. Open with the problem from a human perspective. Use "we" heavily. One bolded sentence at the top that captures the entire point.
- **Process / procedural docs:** Numbered steps, headers per section, scannable. Bold the key takeaway in each section.
- **Presentations / training:** Use frameworks and metaphors. Bold the key takeaways. Mix practical advice with big-picture thinking.
- **Performance / coaching notes:** Person-specific. Lead with their strengths, connect to concrete next steps. No generic advice.
- **Client-facing proposals:** Persuasive but grounded. Lead with the business value, back it with substance.
</details>
